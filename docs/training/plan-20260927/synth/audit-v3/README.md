## Known limitations

1. **Determinacy classes remain proxies.** v3 closes the measured v2 holes
   (comment prose, glue code, invisible package helpers), but `novelty` still
   allows a small budget of unseen identifiers and numbers (base-R functions
   and domain constants), and recombined visible words in strings and
   comments are allowed by design. `sibling` relies on masked 8-gram support.
2. **Supply limits volume.** Medium and long determined cuts are still the
   binding constraint: every surviving medium/long cut is used, and the
   +-3 pp cohort rule binds on the 5-8-line bucket. Long buckets (21-45
   lines) are under-represented in SYNTH alone; the TRAIN+SYNTH cohort stays
   within 3 points of TRAIN in every bucket.
3. **Prompt geometry is closer to TRAIN but not equal.** Median 28 prefix
   lines / 356 prompt tokens against TRAIN's 11 / 229 (v2: 40 / 495); 7.5% of
   prompts have more than 60 prefix lines (TRAIN 3.8%, v2 41%); the 21-40,
   41-60 and >60 prefix buckets sit at the 2x cap, and <=10-line prompts are
   under-represented (19% vs 47%). Prompts without any blank line are 32%
   (TRAIN 16%), mostly short `strip` packagings. `file` is 10% of finish rows.
3a. **Probe.** On 150 fresh v3 cases arm A (Q8_0, greedy) is exact on 15/60
   short, 9/50 medium, 0/10 long finish cases and 1/30 roxygen; the medium
   rate is lower than v2's 16/50 (different seeds and packagings; n=50).
4. **Behavioural coverage is partial; "entered" is not "asserted".** Code
   that needs add-on packages is parse-checked only.
5. **Roxygen prose stays free text**; roxygen is <= 10% and tag-matched.
6. **One author family, one brief per wave**, and pattern-heavy code by
   design: v3 over-represents repetitive structure relative to real code.
7. **Residual contamination risk:** LLM-memorised paraphrase is not
   detectable by the filters. DEV line-4-gram removals are much higher than
   in v2 (templated validation lines are generic); all are dropped.
