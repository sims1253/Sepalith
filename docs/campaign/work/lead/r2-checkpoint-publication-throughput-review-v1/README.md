# Checkpoint publication throughput review

Checkpoint354 serialized and sealed 17,250,977,317 bytes on native storage, then copied, hashed, fsynced, and atomically published them to E. The 24-update compute window took 480.75 seconds from the first to last log. Serialization took 24.42 seconds, sealing took 13.29 seconds, and publication took 160.31 seconds. Save through durable publication therefore added 198.02 seconds before the internal evaluation and terminal verification, about 29% of compute-plus-save time for this window.

The frozen path performs two full native reads before root acceptance: seal inventory and copy-time hashing. The trainer then performs a full E read at terminal verification. The admitted root verifier adds one full native and one full E read. Including serialization and the durable copy, immediate terminal acceptance moves or reads about seven checkpoint payload equivalents. File fsync calls do not intentionally read the payload, although they flush dirty writes.

The smallest later optimization is to keep one independent E readback and remove duplicated verification around it. Root acceptance can use the publisher's source fingerprints for the native copy and hash E only. A later trainer revision can treat its complete publisher receipt as provisional durability and require the root E readback receipt before continuation, avoiding the trainer's immediately preceding E rehash. Neither change applies to checkpoint354 or its admitted verifier.

Cadence24 is appropriate for this recovery checkpoint but expensive as a steady schedule. Based on the observed 20.90-second mean update interval and the 198.02-second save/publish time, estimated checkpoint fractions are 23% at 32 updates, 13% at 64, and 7% at 128 for the same row mix. A 64-update steady cadence keeps the maximum replay below the 91 updates lost in the guard failure. Because cadence is part of the checkpoint identity, this needs a new explicit full-state operational transition after checkpoint354 acceptance.
